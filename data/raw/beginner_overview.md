## Chess for Absolute Beginners (project-authored overview)

Source: Written for this project as a plain-language summary of the standard rules of chess (FIDE Laws of Chess) and common beginner teaching. It is original text, not a quotation from any publication.

## What is chess?

Chess is a two-player strategy board game played on a square board with 64 squares. One player controls the white pieces and the other controls the black pieces, and the players take turns making one move at a time. There is no luck and no hidden information in chess: both players can see the whole board, so the result depends on planning and skill.

The goal of chess is to checkmate the opponent's king, which means attacking it in a way that it cannot escape. A game of chess can also end in a draw, where neither player wins.

Chess is played all over the world at home, in clubs, online and in tournaments. The official rules of chess are maintained by FIDE, the international chess federation.

## How to play chess: the quick version

To play chess, set up the board with each player's 16 pieces and let White move first. The players take turns moving one piece at a time, and each type of piece moves in its own way. You capture an enemy piece by moving onto its square. If your opponent's king is attacked, that is check, and if it cannot escape it is checkmate and you win.

A good way to start is to learn how each piece moves, then learn check and checkmate, then the special moves of castling, en passant and promotion, and finally the opening principles.

## The board and the starting position

The chessboard has eight rows, called ranks, and eight columns, called files. Together they make 64 squares that alternate between light and dark. The board is placed so that each player has a light square in the right-hand corner closest to them.

Each player starts with 16 pieces: one king, one queen, two rooks, two bishops, two knights and eight pawns. The pawns stand on the second row from each player and the other pieces stand behind them. The queen starts on a square of her own colour, so the white queen starts on a light square and the black queen on a dark square. White always makes the first move.

## How each piece moves

The king moves one square in any direction: forwards, backwards, sideways or diagonally. The king can never move to a square where it would be attacked. It is the most important piece because if it is checkmated the game is lost.

The queen is the most powerful piece. She moves any number of squares in a straight line along a rank, a file or a diagonal, as long as no piece is in the way.

A rook moves any number of squares in a straight line along a rank or a file, but not diagonally.

A bishop moves any number of squares diagonally. Because of this, each bishop always stays on squares of the colour it started on.

A knight moves in an L-shape: two squares in one direction and then one square to the side. The knight is the only piece that can jump over other pieces.

A pawn moves forward one square, or two squares from its starting square, and it captures one square diagonally forward. Pawns cannot move backwards. A pawn that reaches the far end of the board is promoted, usually to a queen.

## What the pieces are worth

Beginners use point values as a rough guide to the strength of each piece: a pawn is worth 1 point, a knight 3, a bishop 3, a rook 5 and a queen 9. The king has no point value because losing it ends the game. These numbers are only a guideline, because a strong position can matter more than counting material.

## Check, checkmate and draws

When a king is under attack it is in check. A player in check must get out of check immediately by moving the king, blocking the attack or capturing the attacking piece. It is illegal to leave your own king in check.

If a player is in check and has no way to escape, it is checkmate and the game is over. The player who delivered checkmate wins the game of chess.

A game is a draw if the player to move is not in check but has no legal move; this is called stalemate. A game is also drawn if both players agree to a draw or if neither player can possibly checkmate the other, for example with only the two kings left. A player can also resign, which means giving up and losing the game.

## Castling in plain words

Castling is a special move that helps keep the king safe. The king moves two squares towards a rook and that rook jumps to the other side of the king. You can only castle if the king and that rook have not moved before, there are no pieces between them, the king is not in check, and the king does not pass through or land on a square that is attacked.

## En passant in plain words

En passant is a special pawn capture. If a pawn moves two squares forward from its starting square and lands next to an enemy pawn, that enemy pawn may capture it as if it had moved only one square. This must be done immediately on the very next move or the chance is lost.

## The three phases of a chess game

A game of chess usually has three phases. The opening is the first moves, when players bring their pieces into play and fight for the centre. The middlegame is when most of the tactics and attacks happen. The endgame is when few pieces are left and the kings become active and pawns try to promote.

## Reading chess notation

Chess moves are written in algebraic notation. The files are labelled a to h from left to right and the ranks are numbered 1 to 8 from White's side, so every square has a name such as e4. A move is written with a letter for the piece: K for king, Q for queen, R for rook, B for bishop and N for knight. Pawn moves have no letter. An x means a capture, a plus sign means check, a hash sign means checkmate, and O-O and O-O-O mean castling on the kingside and the queenside.
