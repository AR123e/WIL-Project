## Basic Chess Strategy for Beginners (project-authored)

Source: Written for this project as a plain-language summary of widely taught beginner principles. It is original text, not a quotation from any publication.

## Opening principles in one list

In the opening, try to do four things. Control the centre with pawns or pieces, develop your knights and bishops quickly, castle early so your king is safe and your rook joins the game, and avoid moving the same piece twice without a good reason. Also avoid bringing your queen out too early, because enemy pieces can attack her and gain time.

## Think before every move

Before every move, check three things. First, what is my opponent threatening with their last move? Second, are any of my pieces undefended? Third, can I check, capture or threaten something? Looking at checks, captures and threats in this order stops most beginner blunders.

## Middlegame ideas

In the middlegame, improve your least active piece, put rooks on open files, and keep your king safe. Look for tactics such as forks, pins and skewers, and also check whether your opponent has one. Try to avoid doubled and isolated pawns because they are hard to defend.

## When to trade pieces

If you are ahead in material, trading pieces usually helps you because the extra material counts for more when the board is emptier. If you are behind, avoid trades and look for complications. Never trade without checking that the trade does not leave one of your pieces unprotected.

## Endgame basics

In the endgame the king becomes a strong piece, so bring your king towards the centre. Create a passed pawn and push it. A good rule is to place your rook behind a passed pawn. When you are ahead, be careful not to cause stalemate by leaving the enemy king with no legal move, because that is only a draw.

## Common beginner mistakes

The most common beginner mistakes are moving too fast, leaving pieces hanging, bringing the queen out early, ignoring the opponent's threats, forgetting to castle, pushing too many pawns, and falling for scholar's mate. Slowing down and checking what the opponent just did fixes most of them.

## How to improve at chess

To improve at chess, play regularly, review your games to find your mistakes, solve tactics puzzles every day, and learn the basic endgames. Learn opening principles rather than memorising long lines of moves, because the principles work in every game.
